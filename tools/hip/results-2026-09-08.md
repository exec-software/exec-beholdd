# BF16 rocWMMA routed-MoE results — 2026-09-08

## Outcome

The standalone gfx1151 harness compiled and completed three deep, thermally
guarded runs. Every `N` and both timing envelopes passed the standalone
mechanical-correctness gates:

- GPU architecture exactly `gfx1151`, wave size 32.
- Native `v_wmma_f32_16x16x16_bf16` present in the embedded code object.
- Finite FP32 output.
- Relative L2 error at most `5e-4`.
- Max absolute error at most `1e-3`.
- Two repeated executions bit-identical, validated independently for the
  device-resident and copy-inclusive paths.

The strongest result is the ideal `N=16` high-overlap cohort. Across the three
runs, its resident median was `0.297362–0.310115 ms`, or
`51,593.68–53,806.56 routed_evals_per_second`. The pinned, host-visible envelope
was `0.308380–0.338952 ms`, or `47,204.27–51,884.08 routed_evals_per_second`.

These are single routed-MoE-operator evaluations, not full-model tokens. The
cohort is intentionally ideal: all tokens route once through the same eight
resident experts. It is an ideal-overlap candidate measurement for a
scheduler-filled 16-row WMMA tile, not a characterization of arbitrary sparse
route mixtures.

## Provenance and timing contract

- GPU: Radeon 8060S Graphics, `gfx1151`, wave32.
- Shape: `H=2048`, `F=512`, `K=8`, 8 experts, token rows padded to 16.
- Public rocWMMA headers: `ROCm/rocm-libraries` commit
  `bf1f3c1980e16cd9ceadc96a1d8ab2c72a006125`.
- Compiler/runtime: `the isolated ROCm environment` ROCm `7.13.26183`.
- Compiler: AMD Clang 23.0.0git commit
  `43215c73116c407735c85a180d174f718798c328`, patched build
  `2506c552d8428e2cc1778bef048b20f818e06bb3`.
- Final embedded gfx1151 code-object SHA-256:
  `6b15a7f8edd0da4fbe623260f845e6d79691e774c4befe1293b193f113bbba1c`.
- Resolved HIP runtime:
  `$ROCM_CORE/lib/libamdhip64.so.7`.
- HIP runtime SHA-256:
  `eb25caf1754e031218c5245f2c0aae55e3607a527d9e53d36719b6caba1b4e05`.
- Persistent device allocation: 49 MiB, of which 48 MiB is BF16 weights.
- Five launches per operator: F32-to-BF16/pad, gate+up WMMA, SiLU/up-to-BF16,
  down WMMA, weighted route reduction.
- Warmups: 4 per envelope and `N`.
- Samples: 9 per envelope and `N`.
- Burst: 8 operators per wall-clock sample, with one final stream sync; reported
  times are divided by 8.
- Timing: `std::chrono::steady_clock`; HIP events were not used.
- Envelope order: alternating by `N` to reduce one-sided DVFS/thermal bias.
- `resident` excludes allocations, all H2D/D2H copies, and one-time weight
  preparation/upload.
- `host_visible` adds pinned H2D F32 input, pinned H2D route metadata, and
  pinned D2H FP32 output. It still excludes allocation and weight upload.
- Both include global weight/activation loads and rocWMMA register-fragment
  packing.
- The Matrix-B weights are one-time transposed/prepared BF16 row-major tensors,
  not checkpoint-native layout.

## F32-policy drift validation

After the unchanged GPU pipeline completed the three deep timing runs, the
harness gained a second CPU oracle for current Beholdd numerical semantics:
BF16 weights, original F32 token input, F32 post-SiLU activation, and F32
accumulation/down input. A final guarded all-`N` smoke run compared the GPU
candidate to both references independently.

The candidate's mechanical error against the reference that mirrors its BF16
WMMA boundaries remained `1.097036e-4–1.699449e-4` relative L2 and at most
`3.084715e-7` max absolute. Its actual precision-policy drift against the F32
Beholdd reference was `0.002652090–0.002799975` relative L2 (about
`0.265–0.280%`) and at most `5.584268e-6` max absolute. The policy metric is
informational; it is separate from the mechanical gate.

## Interpretation

The result proves that native BF16 WMMA is available and fast through the ROCm
7.13 wheel on gfx1151, despite system ROCm 5.7 being unable to identify the GPU.
It also shows why scheduler-controlled token coalescing is the promising use:
the padded device work is nearly constant from `N=1` to `N=16`, so useful
routed-evaluation throughput scales with tile occupancy.

This experiment alone does not qualify production integration. A decision must
combine it with an apples-to-apples native control using the same precision
boundaries, prepared weight layout, persistent residency, and wall-clock timing
contract, then test partially overlapping expert cohorts and end-to-end
scheduler cost.
